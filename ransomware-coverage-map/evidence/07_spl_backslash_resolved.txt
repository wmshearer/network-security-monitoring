SPL backslash-dot resolution, tested live against Splunk Enterprise 10.4.2
Date: 2026-08-28

QUESTION: does 'file_name IN ("*\.txt")' match a plainly-named .txt file?
Detection under test: security_content/detections/endpoint/ransomware_notes_bulk_creation.yml

--- A. literal SPL from the detection ---
| makeresults | eval file_name="cHpfiXA9s.README.txt" | search file_name IN ("*\.txt","*\.html","*\.hta")
RESULT: MATCHED (returned the row)

--- B. permissive form, plain dot ---
| makeresults | eval file_name="cHpfiXA9s.README.txt" | search file_name IN ("*.txt")
RESULT: MATCHED

--- C. negative control ---
| makeresults | eval file_name="notes.pdf" | search file_name IN ("*\.txt","*\.html","*\.hta")
RESULT: no match (correct)

--- D. harness note ---
'where ... IN' does NOT wildcard-match; 'search ... IN' does.
An early test used 'where' and returned empty for BOTH readings, which
would have been read as confirming the strict reading. The plain-dot
control caught it: a form that must match returned nothing, proving the
harness was wrong rather than the hypothesis right.

CONCLUSION: Splunk treats the backslash permissively. The strict reading
is WRONG. lockbit_ransomware|T1486 and chaos_ransomware|T1486 are GREEN,
not RED-LOGIC.
